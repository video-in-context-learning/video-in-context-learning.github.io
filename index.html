Video models are in-context learners

Andreas Hochlehnert*, Thaddäus Wiedemer*, Paul Hayes, Thomas Kipf, Been Kim, Robert Geirhos*

* Joint leads
Paper PDF Podcast
TL;DR: Language models progressed from task-specific training to zero-shot prompting and in-context learning. We show that frontier video models such as Gemini Omni 1.1 and Seedance 2.5 are capable of in-context learning too, beating zero-shot performance on visual tasks while requiring no weight updates, adapters, or architectural changes.

Prompt format:

Is this in-context learning, or memorization?

How can we rule out that tasks are simply memorized from training data? The only solution is to create a new task from scratch, after models were trained. We therefore created a challenging color transform, ‘chromatic scramble v1’, which is absent from natural videos. The text prompt states the mathematical rule (including in the zero-shot setting, k = 0), while in-context examples provide additional visual context for k ≥ 1.

Chromatic scramble v1 per-pixel transform on 8-bit RGB channels (R, G, B ∈ {0, …, 255})
Red R′ = 255 − B inverted blue channel (cross-channel inversion) Green G′ = G identical Blue B′ = (R + 128) mod 256 180° color phase shift of red channel

Inference-time scaling: Combining in-context learning with Best-of-N sampling

In-context examples and test-time sampling interact superadditively on Chromatic scramble. Increasing the number of examples lowers error and produces steeper Best-of-N power-law exponents. Within-query rank statistics explain why: worst-attempt error remains near the 100% baseline across shot counts, whereas best-attempt error decreases substantially with more examples.
